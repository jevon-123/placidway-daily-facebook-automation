Addiction Treatment in Tijuana, Mexico: When to Schedule Your Stay

A month-by-month way to protect treatment time, plan a safe return home and leave room for the people and events that matter to you.

The date that almost changed the plan

A wedding on one calendar. Admission on the other.

An American patient planning residential care in Tijuana had circled an admission week near Mexican Independence Day. Her grandchild’s wedding was later in the autumn, so the timing initially looked comfortable. Then she realized she had not confirmed whether intake staff, a prescribing clinician and transportation would all be available during the holiday period. She moved the proposed admission earlier and asked for a written discharge and aftercare plan before making a commitment.

She is a composite patient, not a real person or a report about a particular clinic. Her near-miss illustrates the useful question behind this calendar: not simply which month has pleasant weather, but which start date allows enough time for assessment, any medically necessary withdrawal care, residential treatment and a supported transition back to the United States. If you need urgent help now, do not wait for a preferred season. Ask a clinician about the safest available level of care.

Before choosing a month

Keep two calendars open

One calendar covers your life at home: a job schedule, insurance plan, rent, caregiving and the first weeks after discharge. The other covers care in Tijuana: intake availability, the clinical schedule, Mexican holidays and travel through the San Diego–Tijuana border. Neither calendar should override a medical assessment.

Your U.S. calendar

List the dates you cannot easily move. Include paid time off or leave requests, school pickups, family occasions, insurance renewal, recurring prescriptions and your first appointment with a U.S. clinician after discharge.

If you are driving from Southern California, allow for border crossing time in both directions. If you are flying, account for the trip through San Diego or the airport you and the provider agree to use. Avoid booking an immediate shift at work after your return.

The treatment calendar

Ask when evaluation occurs, whether a physician can assess withdrawal risk before travel, and whether detox is provided on site, arranged elsewhere or unavailable. A quoted “30-day program” may count detox days within the stay or begin after detox.

Confirm who is present overnight, how a medical emergency leads to hospital care and how the team will coordinate medications and follow-up once you are home. A clinic may operate through a holiday while its admissions or administrative team has different hours.

What a treatment timeline actually looks like

Residential addiction treatment is not a trip with a fixed healing interval after one procedure. Admission generally starts with an assessment of substance use, physical health, mental health, medications and withdrawal risk. Some people need medical detox before they can take part fully in therapy. Withdrawal care may take several days or longer, depending on the substance, symptoms and clinician’s assessment. Alcohol and benzodiazepine withdrawal in particular can require close medical supervision. Do not attempt to shorten or skip recommended detox to meet a wedding, flight or return-to-work date.

A residential stay may be planned for about 30 days, while some people benefit from 60 to 90 days or a different level of care. During a stay, ask about individual and group therapy, approaches such as cognitive behavioral therapy or motivational interviewing, treatment for co-occurring mental health conditions and medications when clinically appropriate. A program should explain which of these it actually offers rather than describing every service as universally suitable.

Plan a pair of handoff dates, not just an admission date: your expected discharge from Tijuana and your first confirmed follow-up with a U.S. clinician or treatment service. Ask your treating team how soon that visit should occur. Arrange it before leaving, rather than waiting until you are back at work.

Many patients make one residential treatment trip, followed by care at home. A second trip is not routinely required. If your team recommends a later in-person review in Tijuana, put both travel windows on your calendar and ask what clinical change would make the second visit necessary. There is no standard “healing gap” to fill with a vacation. The period after discharge is an active part of care, with follow-up, medication planning, peer support or outpatient treatment as appropriate.

For opioid use disorder, ask specifically whether evidence-based medication treatment is available and how any prescribed medication would continue in the United States. Do not assume a Mexican prescription can be filled at a U.S. pharmacy or that medication can simply be carried across the border. Discuss the plan with both treating clinicians and check applicable border rules. If withdrawal, intoxication or medication effects make travel unsafe, follow medical advice instead of keeping a prepaid ticket.

Month-by-month strip

A planning cue for every month

These are scheduling considerations, not clinical rankings or predictions of weather, availability or price. Tijuana’s coastal climate is generally milder than inland desert areas, but individual days vary.

January

Allow for cool, sometimes rainy days. Confirm admissions and transport around New Year’s Day and U.S. return-to-work travel.

February

Check schedules around Mexico’s Constitution Day observance and U.S. Presidents’ Day. Ask whether quoted rates change during winter travel periods.

March

Benito Juárez’s birthday observance and spring-break travel can affect staffing or border traffic. Rain is still possible.

April

Holy Week can fall in March or April. Ask the provider about intake, pharmacy access and family travel before booking.

May

Confirm arrangements around Mexico’s Labor Day and U.S. Memorial Day. Coastal mornings may be cloudy even when afternoons are comfortable.

June

Summer vacations begin for many families. Build childcare and household coverage before committing to a residential stay.

July

U.S. Independence Day travel may mean busier routes. Ask a driver or coordinator how return pickup and border crossing will work.

August

Late-summer schedules may suit patients with leave before school resumes. Inland parts of Tijuana can feel warmer than the coast.

September

Plan around U.S. Labor Day and Mexican Independence Day. Verify admission staffing rather than assuming routine holiday hours.

October

Often useful for patients planning ahead of winter family events. Leave time after discharge for appointments and adjustment at home.

November

Ask about Day of the Dead, Mexico’s Revolution Day observance, U.S. Veterans Day and Thanksgiving schedules.

December

Confirm clinical coverage over Christmas and New Year’s travel, plus your U.S. follow-up clinician’s holiday hours.

Winter | December through February

A quieter-looking calendar can still be crowded

Tijuana’s winters tend to be cooler and wetter than its summers. If you expect to spend time outdoors between scheduled activities, bring layers and a rain jacket. Ask whether the residence has comfortable indoor space for rest and therapy rather than treating weather as a reason to delay needed care. Coastal Tijuana and nearby Rosarito are influenced by the ocean, while inland neighborhoods can feel different during the same day.

Christmas, New Year’s Day and Mexico’s Constitution Day observance deserve specific questions about admissions, medical coverage, laboratory access and pharmacy arrangements. U.S. holiday trips can add traffic at San Ysidro and Otay Mesa. A winter admission may also overlap year-end family gatherings. If those gatherings matter to you, discuss a clinically reasonable date with the provider, but do not compress treatment to be home for one meal.

Seasonal promotions or open beds may occur, but neither a discount nor a quiet-looking week establishes what care is available. Compare written quotes for the same clinical needs. If your U.S. job has a busy year-end period, find out whether protected leave may apply and who can cover essential responsibilities. You do not need to share your diagnosis with every coworker to make a practical coverage plan.

Spring | March through May

Check the holiday before you check the airfare

Spring can bring mild days, lingering rain and cool coastal mornings. Holy Week, also called Semana Santa, may fall in March or April and can change travel patterns and business hours. Mexico’s Benito Juárez birthday observance and Labor Day also matter. On the U.S. side, spring break and Memorial Day may affect flights, family availability and roads approaching the border.

If you hope to begin before a summer family event, count backward from the event only after the provider has evaluated the likely treatment length. A 30-day booking is not a promise that you will feel ready for a large celebration immediately after discharge. Decide in advance who can handle invitations, childcare or work messages while your attention is on treatment.

Ask the admissions team to distinguish its office hours from clinical staffing. A scheduled group session continuing through a holiday does not tell you whether a new patient can be assessed or whether a specialist can be reached that day.

A useful spring call

“If I arrive during Holy Week, who completes my medical assessment, who is on site overnight and what happens if I need a higher level of withdrawal care?” Ask for the answers in writing before paying a deposit.

Summer | June through August

Use the longer days, not a shorter treatment plan

Tijuana’s coastal influence often keeps summer more moderate than the inland desert, although an inland facility or an unusually warm spell may feel hotter. Marine cloud or fog can affect mornings near the coast. Confirm the actual address of a proposed residence, its ventilation and its arrangements for people taking medications that affect heat tolerance. Your clinician can advise on medication-specific concerns.

School vacation can make family coverage easier for some adults and much harder for parents or caregivers. U.S. Independence Day may bring heavier leisure travel. If you are coming through San Diego, ask whether pickup is offered at the airport, at a specified border meeting point or only at the facility. Get the pickup location and any fee in writing. Do not assume an advertised airport transfer includes crossing the border with you.

Summer trips may also overlap staff vacations or popular family travel weeks. Availability and rates vary by provider, so request an itemized quote for your intended dates rather than relying on a seasonal price claim. For an autumn wedding or work milestone, summer admission may create more room for discharge planning and early follow-up. The correct duration still comes from your clinical needs, not the event calendar.

Autumn | September through November

Protect the weeks after you come home

Autumn temperatures in Tijuana may remain comfortable, though weather can change and the rainy season approaches later. Early autumn brings U.S. Labor Day and Mexican Independence Day. Later, Day of the Dead, Mexico’s Revolution Day observance and U.S. Thanksgiving can affect travel, appointments or family expectations. Day of the Dead is culturally significant even where ordinary clinical services continue. Ask what changes at the specific provider instead of assuming a full closure.

For many patients, the challenge is not obtaining a bed in autumn. It is finding an available U.S. therapist, prescriber or outpatient program before Thanksgiving and winter holidays fill their schedules. A border crossing may be convenient from California, but proximity does not replace an aftercare appointment. Ask the Tijuana team when it will send your discharge information, with your consent, and who handles medication questions during the handoff.

If a family event follows soon after treatment, you can plan a lower-pressure role: shorter attendance, a trusted ride, a quiet place to step away and permission to decline alcohol-centered activities. There is no obligation to make a celebration the test of your progress. If your clinician advises more treatment or a different level of support, give that advice priority over an RSVP.

Benefit-year timing tip

Check the U.S. plan year before you pay

If you have U.S. insurance, do not assume an overseas residential program is covered. Ask the insurer directly about out-of-country treatment, prior authorization, covered levels of care, deductibles and whether any U.S. aftercare will be in network. A plan-year reset can change what you pay for covered U.S. services before or after your trip. Confirm your own plan’s reset date rather than assuming it follows the calendar year.

Health savings account and flexible spending account rules may allow payment of qualifying medical expenses, including some substance use treatment, but eligibility depends on the expense and the account rules. Ask your account administrator what documentation it requires for treatment outside the United States. FSA deadlines and rollover provisions differ by employer. Do not delay medically necessary care solely to use a balance or meet a deductible.

Budget alongside the calendar

Compare the whole stay, not the advertised bed rate

For planning, an estimated 30-day residential package in Tijuana is approximately $4,000–$15,000. Accommodation, meals and counseling are commonly included, while medical detox, medications, laboratory tests and specialist care may cost extra. These are broad price estimates, not live offers. Clinical scope and total charges must be confirmed directly with each provider in a written quote.

Ranges are approximate and can change with clinical intensity, availability, length of stay and accommodation. They do not establish that one location or provider is safer or better.

A useful quote specifies the number of nights, when the clock starts, assessment fees, physician visits, detox monitoring, laboratory tests, medications, therapy schedule, private-room charges, hospital transfers, transportation, payment schedule and refund terms. Ask what happens to the price if a clinician recommends a longer stay or hospital care. Also ask whether the provider bills in U.S. dollars or Mexican pesos and how exchange-rate changes are handled.

If you are comparing Tijuana with Rosarito, Puerto Vallarta, Cancun or Mexico City, weigh provider qualifications and recognized accreditation, clinical safety and detox capability, U.S. aftercare, total cost, travel access and fit with your treatment and language needs. Do not turn those factors into a destination ranking without checking actual providers. A shorter drive from San Diego can be valuable, but it cannot make up for a missing service your clinician says you need.

The questions worth asking in every season

Make the clinical plan verifiable

Request the facility’s full legal name and address, information about its authorization to operate, and the names and professional credentials of the clinicians who would assess and treat you. Ask how to verify relevant Mexican professional licenses, often called cédulas profesionales. A facility authorization, an individual clinician’s credential and voluntary accreditation are different things. Ask what any accreditation covers rather than treating a logo as proof of medical detox capability.

Describe your substance use and medical history honestly to a qualified clinician before travel, including alcohol or benzodiazepine use, prior withdrawal seizures, pregnancy, chronic illness and prescribed medications where applicable. Ask whether detox is available on site at the level you need, who monitors you overnight, what vital-sign and medication protocols are used, and which hospital receives patients who need emergency care. Request the emergency transfer plan and an explanation of who pays for an ambulance or hospital services.

Privacy is practical as well as personal. Ask who can receive updates, how records are stored, whether rooms are shared, and how your discharge information can be sent securely to a U.S. clinician with your consent. Find out whether therapy and medical discussions are available in English without relying on another patient to interpret. If family participation is offered, ask whether it can be scheduled around work and time zones.

For an immediate medical emergency in Mexico, the emergency number is 911. In the United States, call 911 for an immediate emergency. If you are in the United States and need immediate mental health or substance use crisis support, call or text 988. These services do not replace a planned detox assessment. Your own clinician can help determine whether cross-border residential care is appropriate or whether you need a different setting sooner.

Countdown planner

Work backward from the event, then let clinical advice set the pace

First: put the event and homecoming on separate lines

Mark the wedding, family gathering or work obligation. Separately mark the earliest realistic return home after the treatment length recommended for you. Leave space for your first U.S. follow-up and a manageable return to daily responsibilities. This is planning room, not permission to reduce care.

Next: confirm assessment and the proposed admission window

Speak with a qualified clinician about withdrawal risk before buying nonrefundable travel. Ask the provider whether assessment happens remotely before arrival, at admission or both. If detox is expected, clarify its location, potential duration and relationship to the residential package.

Before paying: place both countries’ holidays beside the dates

Check U.S. travel peaks and Mexican observances against intake staffing, specialist availability, pharmacy access, airport or border pickup and your support person’s schedule. Obtain written confirmation for the services you need, not simply an open-bed notice.

Before departure: reserve the handoff

Book a U.S. follow-up appointment for the timeframe your treatment team recommends. Confirm how you will receive records and medication instructions. If another in-person visit to Tijuana is advised, tentatively pair its travel dates with that U.S. appointment and confirm later whether the visit remains clinically needed.

After discharge: keep the calendar adjustable

Recovery needs and treatment recommendations can change. Keep your support contacts, transport alternatives and leave options available rather than treating the original return date as a medical deadline.

Family-event checklist

Keep the celebration from becoming a deadline

  • Tell one trusted person what practical help you want while away, such as childcare, pet care or handling invitations. Share medical details only as you choose.
  • Ask your clinician what level of activity and support makes sense after discharge. Consider a shorter appearance or a way to leave early.
  • Set up transportation that does not depend on you driving if you are unwell, fatigued or affected by medication.
  • Keep follow-up appointments even when a celebration, holiday or first day back at work competes for the same time.
  • Give yourself permission to miss an event if the recommended treatment plan requires it. Your place in your family is not measured by one date on the calendar.

The best month is one that supports the full plan

Tijuana’s access from the United States can make an extended stay easier to arrange, especially if you live near Southern California. Still, a workable admission date must match the care you need, the provider’s verified capabilities, your finances and a real handoff at home. Weather and holiday travel are useful planning details, not reasons to postpone urgent assessment or to leave treatment early.

The patient at the start of this page is a composite created to show how an event and a holiday can intersect. Her plan is not a prediction of anyone else’s treatment course. Bring your own clinician into the decision, request a written provider-specific quote and keep both calendars flexible.

Get the dates and inclusions in writing

Share your preferred admission window, expected length of stay and U.S. follow-up needs. Ask for provider-specific details about licensing, detox, emergency coverage, holiday staffing, transportation and charges before deciding.

Request a Tijuana treatment schedule and itemized quote

A request for information does not replace medical assessment or commit you to a start date.
